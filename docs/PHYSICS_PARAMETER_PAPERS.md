# Learning organ mechanics from interaction videos: primary-paper review

Research cutoff: **7 October 2026**. Scope: deformable organ/tool contact, constitutive identification, differentiable simulation and visual reconstruction. Twelve papers were selected for complementary evidence, not ranked by venue or image quality. Full texts, publisher records and official repositories were checked; the force-patch paper is abstract-only and several supporting references have narrower access. The machine-readable companion is [paper_matrix.json](../references/physics/paper_matrix.json), with [CSV](../references/physics/paper_matrix.csv) for comparison. No third-party implementation was benchmarked locally for this review.

The best defensible architecture is **calibrated observations → low-dimensional inverse mechanics → independently tested contact simulation → detailed rendering**. Ordinary surgical video can constrain motion, geometry and relative response. It does not, by itself, guarantee patient-specific Young’s modulus, viscosity or friction. A model can reproduce the observed motion with incorrect material parameters: DiffPD demonstrates this directly. Visual quality and mechanical identification must have separate acceptance criteria. [DiffPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05917)

“Identifiable target” means the quantities attempted under that paper’s assumptions; it does not assert that every parameter is uniquely recovered in every scene.

## Critical distinctions for VISCERA

**An accurate-looking deformation is not a measured material law.** For a quasistatic linear contact model, `F = k·δ`, scaling both unknown `F` and `k` by the same positive factor leaves the observed displacement unchanged. In the dynamic model `m·δ̈ + c·δ̇ + k·δ = F`, a calibrated mass and sufficiently resolved transient motion may constrain `k/m` and `c/m` only with known/parameterized forcing or verified free-decay conditions; arbitrary unknown `F(t)` can compensate for those coefficients; arbitrary mass choices set a model convention rather than measuring tissue density. If all of `m`, `c`, `k` and `F` are free, their common scale is again ambiguous. This is a model-level derivation, not a claim that every inverse problem has the same ambiguity. [ElastNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8346903/), [laparoscopic model, §§3.3–3.4.5 and 5](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/13/8/863)

Spring stiffness `k [N/m]` depends on contact area, organ shape, support, mesh and discretization. It is not Young’s modulus `E [Pa]`. Likewise, a dashpot `c [N·s/m]`, continuum viscosity `η [Pa·s]`, Rayleigh damping and numerical damping are distinct. A clinical material record must declare the constitutive model, units, loading rate, strain range, tissue state and calibration assumptions. An editor “softness” control can be useful while remaining an engineering control.

The 2026 laparoscopic paper is especially relevant, but its full methods qualify its headline. It normalizes depth before backprojection, fixes 0.001 kg per surface vertex and prescribes a loading/release schedule. The authors state that metric scaling is not propagated into the mechanical outputs and formal identifiability remains unproven. Treat its three outputs as scene/model response estimates. The reported 1.6 ms is the parameter head, not the whole reconstruction pipeline. [Primary paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/13/8/863)

A force sensor is one calibration option, not the only one. A known reference material, calibrated actuator/load, or informative dynamics with known mass/density can provide mechanical scale. Each route needs its own uncertainty budget. Friction is dimensionless and can sometimes be constrained from slip geometry/dynamics; it still needs tangential excitation and an explicit contact/support model. A stationary normal indentation alone will not establish sliding friction. [Calibrated patch](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39873683/), [DiffTactile](https://difftactile.github.io/static/pdf/paper.pdf)

Spatial resolution of rendering is separate from resolution of mechanical evidence. A dense neural material field can encode prior assumptions far more finely than the observations constrain. Start with a small number of regions and parameters, then increase complexity only when additional loading modes materially improve identification. A bi-layer indentation study independently supports designing the observation modalities and sensitivity analysis before increasing constitutive complexity. [Fougeron et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmbbm.2024.106572)

## Recommended incremental architecture

The following is a proposed design, not a claim that the cited papers already supply a validated organ simulator.

1. **Observation contract.** Save native frames/timestamps, camera intrinsics/distortion, stereo/extrinsics or independent scale reference, depth/track confidence, tool geometry/pose, contact segmentation, visible tissue tracks, force/torque if available, reference state and support assumptions. Keep raw observations alongside derived values. Specularities, smoke, blood and tool occlusion become explicit masks/uncertainty, not invented geometry.
2. **Mechanics separate from rendering.** Build a coarse volumetric domain for solid organs, with a bound detailed surface for visualization. Shell/hollow-organ behavior needs a different domain including thickness and internal pressure; a surface-only liver proxy cannot silently stand in for every organ. Preserve material coordinates and boundary attachments through deformation.
3. **Low-dimensional calibration first.** Fit a fixed, documented constitutive family and a small number of regional coefficients. Fix or externally constrain weakly observable density/Poisson ratio. Estimate support/contact nuisance parameters separately or profile over plausible values. Use positive physical parameterizations and a loss scaled by measurement uncertainty.
4. **Rate dependence and friction only with excitation.** Gather multiple indentation depths and speeds, hold/relaxation intervals and complete release. Add tangential slide/twist at varied normal loading before estimating friction. Start with one relaxation branch; add anisotropy or multiple time scales only when measurements support them.
5. **Uncertainty and falsification.** Run synthetic recovery using a different mesh/time step than the fitting model, multistart optimization, sensitivity/SVD or profile-likelihood checks and nuisance-parameter sweeps. Check convergence with mesh and time step, energy behavior, penetration and element inversion. An excellent image fit with a flat material likelihood must return “not identified.”
6. **Independent prediction.** Reserve whole interactions, locations, loading directions/rates and specimens. Evaluate force/torque error where measured, 3D displacement, contact timing, relaxation, volume change and numerical validity. Split by specimen/session; adjacent frames are not independent validation. Novel camera reconstruction and novel physical response are separate tests.
7. **Learn amortization after data exist.** Train a video/geometry encoder to propose low-dimensional parameter distributions, followed by inverse-solver refinement. For richer material discovery, investigate the Neural-DFEM energy architecture with calibrated reaction data. Do not train disease→mechanics labels from generated videos or literature averages and present them as patient truth.
8. **Editor contract.** Expose `measured_calibrated`, `effective_motion_fit`, and `engineering_preset` provenance. Display units only when supported. Disease morphology/texture and mechanical evidence remain separate editable attributes, with explicit uncertainty and seed/provenance bindings.

Differentiable FEM is the first continuum route to evaluate for slow tool contact. PhysTwin-style springs are a useful fast prototype and renderer-control model, with explicitly model-dependent coefficients. MPM/Gaussian methods are useful for visual/large-deformation experiments but do not remove calibration requirements. Cutting adds damage, topology change and fracture evidence; it should follow validated non-destructive contact.

## Immediate prototype baseline

Implement a tiny, **non-clinical contact-response benchmark** before a neural model. For calibrated indentation displacement `δ(t)` and measured force `F(t)`, fit nonnegative `k` and `c` in `F = k·δ + c·δ̇ + b` on contact intervals; estimate/remove sensor bias carefully. Smooth differentiation with a documented filter and propagate noise. A rank-deficient design matrix, almost constant speed or missing loading/release should invalidate unsupported coefficients. Its output is effective contact stiffness/damping, not Young’s modulus/viscosity. This Kelvin–Voigt baseline cannot reproduce stress relaxation during a held displacement; that requires a separately justified relaxation model.

The acquired chicken-skin pilot is **not eligible for this fit** merely because it contains force and pose. Its [readiness record](../physics-releases/source-pilot/calibration-readiness.json) fails registration, contact, reference-geometry and model-admissibility checks; the source reports plastic deformation. No k/c, relaxation-branch or Young-modulus estimates are promoted from it.

Use three lanes: synthetic data with known coefficients for unit/sign/rank tests; instrumented phantom/ex-vivo episodes for real force-plus-motion prediction; uninstrumented surgical clips for descriptive normalized motion/decay only, without calling decay material relaxation unless release/contact assumptions are verified. Hold out complete trajectories and compare against constant-stiffness and elastic-only baselines. Validate frame-time and length-unit conversion and demonstrate the force/stiffness scale ambiguity deliberately. No unmeasured clinical parameter label should be emitted.

The next increment is a small known-geometry FEM indentation experiment with one elastic parameter and known load/kinematics, then a single viscoelastic branch. Compare fitted parameters with an independent material measurement and predict a withheld contact site/rate. This is a realistic CPU/Colab entry point; exact runtimes should be measured after implementation, not borrowed from unrelated papers.

## Supporting work and research gaps

- [PhysGaussian, CVPR2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Xie_PhysGaussian_Physics-Integrated_3D_Gaussians_for_Generative_Dynamics_CVPR_2024_paper.html) is a strong forward MPM/rendering reference with supplied material parameters; it is not a demonstrated organ material identifier.
- [gradSim, ICLR2021](https://openreview.net/forum?id=c_E8kFWfhp0) is an earlier differentiable video/simulation reference with controlled assumptions; legacy dependencies and code terms need care.
- [Oddes and Solav, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmbbm.2023.105708) and [indentify](https://github.com/SolavLab/indentify) provide useful synthetic indentation sensitivity machinery. The repository is AGPL-3.0 and requires MATLAB/FEBio-related tooling.
- The [2023 gravitational-loading VFM paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10565362/) is a relevant alternative-calibration lead. Full text access failed in this review; its detailed protocol and runtime are not used as verified implementation specifications.

Remaining gaps are measured tool-contact force/deformation across organ specimens and disease states, independently checked geometry/boundaries, multi-rate loading, reliable friction protocols and material-law validation under new loads. None of the reviewed evidence establishes a general, clinically calibrated all-organ material model learned from arbitrary monocular surgical video.
