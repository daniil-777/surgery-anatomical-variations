# Liver physics: method selection and evidence boundaries

Reviewed 2026-10-07. This is a targeted, multi-venue primary-source review, not a claim to have exhausted every conference or every liver video. The companion mechanics review, video evidence ledger and trained-model report contain the experimental detail and final release decisions.

## What the methods actually establish

| Method / venue | Relevant evidence | Decision for this implementation |
|---|---|---|
| [Stable Neo-Hookean Flesh Simulation, TOG 2018](https://www.tkim.graphics/NEO/StableNeoHookean2018.pdf) | A robust continuum hyperelastic energy; graphics flesh simulation is not a measured liver constitutive calibration. | Use a rest-stable energy with correct small-strain moduli, retain numerical safeguards and report their activation. |
| [Constraint-based stable Neo-Hookean materials, MIG 2021](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/neohookean.pdf) | Connects compliant position constraints to continuum material energies. | Candidate browser replacement for calibrated edge springs. Actual skinny anatomical cages, rest behavior and throughput decide promotion. |
| [Parallel block Neo-Hookean XPBD, MIG 2022](https://profs.etsmtl.ca/sandrews/pdf/xpbdBlockNeo_MIG22_preprint.pdf) | Coupled constraints improve an iterative mechanics solve. | Evaluate coupling; a mathematically correct element formula alone does not guarantee a stable full mesh. |
| [Vertex Block Descent, SIGGRAPH/TOG 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06321v4) | Optimizes implicit-Euler incremental energy with local three-coordinate vertex updates. Limited iterations remain approximate; penalty contact permits overlap. | Selected as the default continuum integration after coupled XPBD and scalar-energy XPBD jittered on the actual cages. Our serial CPU implementation adds energy backtracking and Jacobian bounds; it does not reproduce the paper’s GPU performance. |
| [Augmented Vertex Block Descent, SIGGRAPH/TOG 2025](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/) | Augmented-Lagrangian extensions address hard constraints and high stiffness ratios. | Relevant next contact/capsule solver candidate; not implemented or benchmarked in this release. |
| [Human liver aspiration, Medical Image Analysis 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18171633/) | Twenty-three in-vivo measurements on six healthy human livers, with inverse constitutive fitting and experimental uncertainty. | Biological evidence requires defined loading and measurement uncertainty. The published experimental setup is not equivalent to our free floor scene; no unavailable raw observations are fabricated. |
| [Human capsule/parenchyma model, MICCAI 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18982669/) | Inverse analysis accounts for initial deformation and a capsule over nonlinear viscoelastic parenchyma. Abstract inspected. | Supports treating capsule and prestress as explicit unresolved components, rather than silently fitting them into one stiffness scalar. |
| [PAC-NeRF, ICLR 2023](https://xuan-li.github.io/pdf/publications/li2023pacnerf.pdf) | Multi-view inverse mechanics integrates differentiable MPM and rendering. Its preprocessing specifies static cameras with known intrinsics/extrinsics. | Useful future route for calibrated multi-view inversion. Do not treat arbitrary laparoscopic footage as satisfying its acquisition assumptions. |
| [PhysGaussian, CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Xie_PhysGaussian_Physics-Integrated_3D_Gaussians_for_Generative_Dynamics_CVPR_2024_paper.html) | Continuum MPM evolves Gaussian appearance and material state together. Demonstrates broad material dynamics. | Relevant for future representation changes. Replacing the current mesh/rendering stack does not supply missing liver forces, capsule properties or attachment measurements. |
| [PhysDreamer, ECCV 2024](https://physdreamer.github.io/) | Distils video-generation dynamics priors and assesses perceived interaction realism through a user study. | Useful visual prior or perceptual comparator. Generated motion will not be used as measured liver material labels. |
| [NeuMA, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08257) | Combines physical priors with learned material corrections and particle-driven Gaussian rendering. Primary abstract screened. | Relevant richer material-learning candidate. Learned corrections would still need objectivity, passivity, held-out loading and liver-specific data checks before adoption. |
| [PhysTwin, ICCV 2025](https://jianghanxiao.github.io/phystwin-web/) | Sparse interaction videos reconstruct spring-mass physics, geometry and Gaussian appearance; reported objects include ropes, cloth, plush and packages. Author project and method overview inspected. | Useful for interaction-conditioned inverse modeling and motion comparisons. It does not establish a calibrated liver continuum; adopting a spring-mass digital twin would not resolve our material-transfer discrepancy. |
| [PhysWorld, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21447) | Uses MPM material selection/identification to generate demonstrations for a physics-conditioned graph-network predictor. Primary abstract screened. | Candidate for a later fast surrogate after the forward liver teacher is calibrated. Synthetic teacher accuracy and long-horizon stability must be tested separately from speed. No paper speedup is transferred to this browser. |
| [JIGGLE, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09743) | Differentiable simulation and active sensing estimate tissue attachment boundaries; animal-tissue experiments use stereo observations. | Boundary conditions deserve explicit estimation and testing, not absorption into an arbitrary stiffness increase. Current floor scene remains an explicitly free body. |
| [Physics-Informed Neural Operators for Tissue Elasticity Reconstruction, MICCAI 2025](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/paper/2595_paper.pdf) | Operator learning for MRE inversion evaluated on simulated boxes and abdomen data. MRE provides a different measurement regime from surgical tool manipulation. | Borrow shared inverse mapping and noise evaluation principles. Do not transfer reported MRE errors into claims about laparoscopic force response. |
| [Physics-augmented finite-strain incompressible viscoelasticity, CMAME 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02959v1) | Constrains energy, dissipation and internal variables through generalized standard materials. Its experimental examples are VHB polymers, not liver. | Strong candidate for learning nonlinear multi-time-scale response when sufficiently rich mechanical data exist. A single small-strain SLS history cannot identify that entire law. |
| [Lagrangian-coupled 3DGS–SPH, October 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04336) | Reports synthetic forward/inverse solid-mechanics examples including a liver-shaped object. Screened from the primary abstract. | Track as a recent candidate; a liver-shaped numerical example is not independent biological validation. |

## Selected engineering approach

Retain separate appearance/shape and physics models. Train a bounded, protocol-conditioned inverse neural ensemble from declared synthetic stress/strain histories, refine its proposal against the constitutive response, and calibrate marginal intervals on a separate synthetic split. Report the neural-only estimate and a non-neural fitting comparator. An accurate fit may still be non-identifiable; block application when excitation, sensitivity, residual or support checks fail.

Transfer the fitted instantaneous Young modulus and relaxation parameters into a continuum law with known units and objective material history. Compare the interactive integration with the offline constitutive function rather than assuming identical behavior from similar parameter names. The browser prototype retains the existing convex cage and contact approximations; remeshing, capsule, attachments and full jaw collision are distinct uncompleted model components.

The scalar one-branch SLS estimator remains a small-strain model. Varied excitation improves coverage and diagnostics but does not turn it into a learned large-strain liver law. More complex alternatives can produce nearly indistinguishable curves over a short observation window. Test and publish that ambiguity instead of naming every accepted curve in-distribution biological tissue.

## Evaluation hierarchy

1. **Material equations:** objectivity, stress-free rest, finite-difference gradients, positive parameters, tangent Young/bulk moduli, time history and relaxation, agreement with the offline implementation.
2. **Numerical mechanics:** actual male/female liver cages; rest, gravity, floor, grab/release, pressure and impulse; positive element Jacobians, local/global volume, constraints and safeguards, step/time/mesh sensitivity, simulation-to-wall-time ratio.
3. **Learning:** specimen-disjoint training/validation/calibration/test; separate parameter-region challenge; noise, force/strain scale, rate and history shifts; neural/refined/non-neural baselines; interval coverage **and width**, false rejection and mismatch acceptance.
4. **Observed motion:** review recordings with timestamps and sampled frames; use image-space motion only where scale is absent. Separate rigid movement from deformation. Recovering a force law requires sufficient load, camera, support and specimen information.
5. **Biological agreement:** independent matched force and 3D-motion experiments, with human, porcine, cadaver, phantom and unrelated tissue kept distinct. This level is not established by the current synthetic training.

Bounding-box height is affected by rigid rotation and spreading. It is not a stand-alone incompressibility metric. Render FPS is not simulated time. A low stress fitting residual does not establish uniqueness of material parameters, and numerical convergence does not establish surgical realism.

## Search scope and limits

The searches covered computer vision/graphics (CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ICLR, NeurIPS, SIGGRAPH/TOG, MIG and SCA), medical/surgical computing (MICCAI, IPCAI/IJCARS and robotic deformable manipulation), and tissue/material mechanics journals (including CMAME and Journal of Biomechanics), plus primary dataset/project releases. Queries combined liver, capsule, viscoelasticity, indentation, relaxation, differentiable simulation, material identification, deformable tracking and video. Primary papers and official datasets were preferred. The video report records which clips were actually downloaded and visually inspected; descriptions or abstracts are never counted as viewed videos.

Full-text inspection included the PAC-NeRF assumptions/method, MICCAI operator reconstruction paper, the CMAME PANN paper, and VBD’s energy, local solve and contact sections. Other source depth is recorded in the companion reviews. Coverage is bounded by accessible material and available acquisition metadata. No unobserved patient force measurements, clinical validations or exhaustive-internet coverage are claimed.
