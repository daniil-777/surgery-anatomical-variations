# Surgical photograph to organ material: practical method selection

Research date: **2026-10-08**. Scope: transfer visible tissue colors from a surgical RGB photograph to VISCERA's existing organ materials. This is a research recommendation, not a report that the external models below were run. Implementation acceptance and measurements must be recorded separately.

## Decision

Ship a **reviewed organ mask → robust observed-color estimate → bounded material color override** first. Keep segmentation/organ identity, observed color, inferred albedo, and authored surface detail as separate pieces of evidence. This is the narrowest useful pipeline that can work on a user's single image without claiming to recover unseen anatomy or true tissue optics.

For an image of a liver, the useful operation is: select the liver, inspect the selected pixels, reject obvious glare/shadow/foreign pixels, estimate a representative color and small palette, preview that color on the existing liver, and apply it with undo. The material's existing spatial grain, wetness, roughness, relief, vessels, and mechanical properties should survive unless the user separately changes them. Single-image color transfer does not establish those properties.

**Engineering judgment:** robust statistics are preferable to a generative material model for the initial feature because every transferred color can be traced to reviewed pixels, the process runs locally, and its failures can be measured. This is a synthesis of existing methods, not a claim of a new scientifically validated estimator or superiority on surgical benchmarks.

## What the image can establish

A photo contains surface reflectance mixed with lighting, viewing direction, exposure, white balance, and camera processing. Wet tissue adds specular reflections. Intrinsic decomposition models attempt to separate these effects, but their answer depends on assumptions and learned priors. In a directly relevant surgical approach, **EndoPBR (WACV 2026)** constrains lighting and BRDF estimation using known scene geometry and camera poses; it does not take one arbitrary photo and an unrelated atlas organ as sufficient input. [EndoPBR, final conference version](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Han_EndoPBR_Photorealistic_Synthetic_Data_for_Surgical_3D_Vision_via_Physically-based_WACV_2026_paper.html)

Endoscopic color is also affected by acquisition and display conditions. The medical color-consistency consensus explains the importance and difficulty of establishing faithful color, while a laparoscopic hyperspectral study obtains reflectance using white/dark references and explicitly handles glare. These are useful evidence that a JPEG's RGB values should be called **observed appearance**, not calibrated reflectance. [Medical color consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4305059/), [Laparoscopic spectral imaging validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11321365/)

The mask provides a spatial selection. An automatically proposed mask is not proof of organ identity; its intended organ must be visible in the UI and correctable. Color alone should not be used to decide liver versus spleen, healthy versus diseased tissue, or exposed tissue versus blood. A whole-image average would blend organs, tools, background, and glare into a color that may represent none of them.

## Methods compared

| Method | What existing evidence supports | Fit for this feature |
|---|---|---|
| Reinhard statistical transfer | Transfers image color statistics using a simple transform; an inexpensive classical baseline. | Good baseline, but full-image moments and ordinary variance are easily influenced by contamination. Use organ masks and robust location/scale; do not copy an image's entire illumination range into albedo. [Original publication](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/color-transfer-between-images/) |
| Masked robust CIELAB / OKLab summary | OKLab gives inexpensive lightness/opponent chroma transforms, including published conversion code. Robust medians/quantiles are an implementation choice here. | Recommended first implementation. Traceable pixel selection, low complexity, bounded controls. Neither color space removes illumination. [OKLab author's derivation and code](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) |
| Histogram or optimal-transport transfer | Regularized/relaxed transport addresses multimodal palettes and unequal mass; POT supplies executable EMD and Sinkhorn color-adaptation examples. | Useful when transferring several genuine tissue colors, but extra modes can be tool, blood, glare, or shadow contamination. Do not force all selected-image modes into every organ. [Regularized transport paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5551), [POT example](https://pythonot.github.io/auto_examples/domain-adaptation/plot_otda_color_images.html) |
| ModFlows, AAAI 2025 | Learned invertible RGB transforms based on rectified flows; author implementation and checkpoints exist, with support for large images. | Contemporary color-style baseline, not surgical material estimation. | IntrinsicReal, 2025 preprint / 2026 publication listing | Adapts IntrinsicAnything to real objects using selected pseudo-label albedos; explicitly addresses the synthetic-to-real gap. | Relevant warning against assuming generic object performance transfers to wet tissue. Its separation of observed versus generated regions is a useful design principle. No runnable official release was located in this search. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18900v1), [CVPR version](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Wu_MatMart_Material_Reconstruction_of_3D_Objects_via_Diffusion_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf) |
| EndoPBR, WACV 2026 | Learns surgical-scene material and camera-related lighting with known geometry/poses; evaluates synthetic data for downstream surgical 3D vision. | Best-matched future route when calibrated multiview images and matching geometry are available. These sources do not establish one universally best method for uncalibrated surgical-photo-to-atlas transfer. The practical choice depends on input information: a reviewed single photo supports appearance matching; posed multiview observations with matching geometry support an inverse-rendering experiment.

## Proposed pipeline and specific improvements

The following is a proposed engineering design, with thresholds to be validated rather than presented as medically derived constants.

1. **Decode and normalize safely.** Bound encoded and decoded image size; apply EXIF orientation before mask coordinates; convert supported color profiles to sRGB and record the assumption when a profile is absent. Never average encoded sRGB when performing multiplicative shading calculations: linearize first. Use the same sRGB/linear conventions already present in `atlas_materials.py`.
2. **Obtain one reviewed mask per organ.** A point/box prompted model may propose the selection; a drawn polygon or imported mask is a fully functional deterministic fallback. Show the original photo with an overlay, editable organ label, and excluded pixels. Record whether identity was user assigned or model proposed. Mask coordinates must refer to the decoded, oriented image dimensions.
3. **Select useful interior pixels.** Erode narrow boundaries to reduce surrounding-organ contamination. Reject clipped pixels, very dark pixels, and bright near-neutral candidates likely to be glare. Keep the original rejected-pixel counts. Thresholds are exposure-sensitive heuristics; pale tissue is not automatically glare. Reject the analysis if too few usable pixels remain instead of quietly returning a default color.
4. **Estimate an observed-color target.** Compute a robust center in CIELAB or OKLab, with trimmed ranges and an optional small palette. Estimate hue/chroma separately from lightness and expose brightness-transfer strength. Do not use gray-world white balance on a frame dominated by reddish tissue: its neutral-scene assumption is inappropriate. A known calibration patch is a separate, better source of white-balance information.
5. **Apply a bounded color edit.** An optional base-color override is the smallest coherent addition to the existing recipe. Blend from the previous base to the robust reference center; retain the existing spatial material field. If extending to distribution transfer, map a bounded scalar authored pigment field through a smooth 3–5 color palette, or use a regularized color-only transform with bounded singular values. Do not estimate physical pattern size from unscaled pixels.
6. **Persist evidence and make rollback exact.** Store algorithm version, image checksum, oriented dimensions, organ mapping, selection provenance, selected/accepted/excluded counts, color space, representative swatches, brightness policy, and transfer strength with the recipe. Undo should restore the entire prior recipe. Original photographs need not be embedded in exported GLBs; attach compact provenance and the transfer recipe.

The improvement over unmasked Reinhard is primarily **better evidence selection and control of what can change**, not a more elaborate neural network. A robust center resists small contaminating regions; separate brightness control avoids forcing shadow intensity into base color; unchanged material fields retain known rendering behavior.

There is a recent surgical highlight-removal model that explicitly separates detection from restoration and then uses LaMa inpainting. Its **detection output** is relevant to future exclusion masks. Synthesized restoration pixels should not count as measured reference colors. [MICCAI 2025 paper](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/0029-Paper0536.html), [Author code](https://github.com/tkllndxn/highlight-removal)

## Integration with the inspected VISCERA material system

At research time, `py/viscera_core/atlas_materials.py` defines a frozen `MaterialRecipe`, computes `uAtlasBaseColor` in linear sRGB, and exposes shared CPU/WebGL material sampling. Its profiles explicitly describe authored visual controls. The field has independent wetness, fine detail, structure, roughness, and a ligament color. These are favorable integration points: a serializable optional color override can alter base color while preserving existing CPU/shader sampling, texture baking, and export behavior.

The transfer should be scoped to the selected organ's tissue material. It must not recolor other organs or the distinct ligament region as a side effect. A single global organ color is an **appearance reference**, not a spatially aligned photographic texture. Projecting vessel positions or lesions from the photo would require anatomical correspondence and matching geometry; it must be a distinct later capability.

Suggested user-facing labels are **Reference photo**, **Selected organ**, **Review selection**, **Reference color**, **Match brightness**, and **Apply color**. A concise limitation can state: “Matches colors visible in this photo; lighting affects the result.” Keep provenance and detailed diagnostics available in an expandable analysis panel or exported metadata.

## Validation that would justify acceptance

| Test | Required observation | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic known-color tissue with inserted white highlights, dark shadows, and blue/gray tool regions | Under frozen contamination cases, robust masked center has lower chroma error than unmasked RGB mean and ordinary moments; report errors, retained fraction, and failures | Generalization to real surgical images |
| Bright pale tissue, near-black tissue, and uniformly saturated image | Pale tissue is not entirely discarded; unreliable/empty selections fail clearly; no NaN or fabricated default | Accurate glare classification on every organ |
| Mask boundary contamination and small/disconnected masks | Boundary exclusion is measurable, invalid geometry is rejected, no outside-organ pixels silently enter statistics | Automatic organ recognition |
| Exact same image under a scalar exposure change | Report chroma/brightness sensitivity separately; brightness-preserving mode behaves as designed | True color invariance under unknown white balance |
| Image normalization | Orientation, dimensions, alpha, color profile, and downsampling preserve overlay alignment; analysis agrees with displayed source | Camera calibration |
| Recipe round trip and undo | Serialization/export retains selected color and provenance; undo restores the prior recipe exactly | Optical truth |
| Preservation | Mesh vertices, faces, organ identity, physics parameters, vessel layout, roughness, and relief are unchanged by color-only apply | Clinical suitability |
| CPU/GPU/export agreement | Existing material tests plus selected-color samples agree within declared numerical tolerance | Correct real-world BRDF |
| Actual browser flow | Upload → select → review overlay/swatches → apply → rotate → undo; visible color changes without shader or loading failures | Real surgical mask quality if the image is synthetic |
| Held-out real-image audit | Independently reviewed organ masks; report mask IoU/Dice, usable-pixel fraction, color consistency, and reviewer failures by organ/procedure | Calibrated reflectance unless acquisition ground truth exists |

Do not set acceptance thresholds after looking at the result. Keep synthetic algorithm tests, real-image engineering examples, trained-model benchmarks, and expert review as separate evidence tiers. A handful of visually pleasing images can demonstrate the workflow but cannot support a “best method” claim.

## Next research step after the usable baseline

Build a small acquisition-controlled dataset of the **same tissue or realistic phantom** across camera distances, light intensities, exposure settings, and viewpoints, including a color reference captured under matched conditions. Compare the robust baseline, masked statistical transfer, regularized color transport, and an intrinsic-decomposition candidate on repeated color consistency and held-out views. Where calibrated geometry and poses exist, compare an EndoPBR-inspired optimizer with fixed zero metalness, explicit illumination, and conservative tissue priors. Treat recovered roughness as an estimate requiring its own validation.

Until then, generic diffusion output may be an optional visual proposal, but should not replace the reviewed pixels as evidence. No pretrained inverse-rendering weights were downloaded or incorporated for this research note.

## Search coverage and confidence

Primary sources inspected include author repositories and license files, author papers on arXiv, CVF's 2025–2026 proceedings, MICCAI 2025, author color-space documentation, POT documentation, and medical imaging studies. Searches covered classical and current color transfer, intrinsic decomposition, surgical specularity, calibration, and surgical inverse rendering. Secondary search results were used to discover sources, not as evidence for method claims.

Confidence is **high** in the distinction between observed color and calibrated material, **high** in the implementation feasibility of a masked robust color override, and **low/unestablished** in performance on unseen real surgical photos until the above audit is run. This is a focused current survey, not an exhaustive systematic review. Model release and license status are the pages observed on the research date; pin exact revisions before future adoption.
