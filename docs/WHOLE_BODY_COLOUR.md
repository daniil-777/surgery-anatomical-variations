# Whole-body colour from surgical photographs

Implemented 8 October 2026. **Whole scene → Whole-body colour** builds one reviewed colour plan from one or several photographs and assigns it to the registered body structures. It keeps the existing surface textures, wetness, roughness, organ geometry and simulation state. This is an observed-colour workflow, not recovery of calibrated tissue optics.

## Use

For the default set, open **Whole scene → Whole-body colour → Apply default library materials**. This single action loads the bundled photographs and reviewed masks, analyses every region, and applies all **36 soft-organ materials** using 65% colour-only transfer with related-tissue borrowing and gentle harmonization enabled. No region selection or review checkboxes are needed. The progress bar tracks completed image loads, analyses and application; a green checkmark and **All 36 organ materials applied** appear when the scene accepts the complete palette. Six organ types are sampled directly and the other assignments remain labelled inferred.

The automatic action always uses the original library assignments, independently of edited or uploaded custom drafts. It commits only after every source region succeeds. Cancellation or failure preserves the previous materials; undo restores them and clears the completion checkmark. The applied plan and source provenance remain downloadable. An automatic-library note records how the inputs were accepted, without marking custom inputs as manually reviewed.

To customize your own palette, use the **Custom references** controls below that action:

1. Open **Whole scene → Whole-body colour → Load reference library**, or add your own PNG, JPEG or WebP images. The library contains six original human laparoscopic photographs with seven final expert-reviewed masks, covering six organ classes. The first photograph contains separate liver and stomach regions; a brighter second liver view supplements the shadowed view.
2. For an uploaded photograph, add a tissue region, assign its tissue identity and target structures, and draw its bounds or add corrective points. Several regions can share an image. Identify whether the visible tissue is an exterior, mucosa, cut surface or uncertain/mixed surface. These are materially different appearances.
3. Choose **Analyse all regions**. Original reviewed masks are used for the bundled photographs. Uploaded regions use the configured local SAM2.1 model, or the explicit classical fallback. The API decodes each image once and evaluates its regions separately. A failed region stays visibly failed; successful regions remain available for inspection.
4. Inspect each overlay, palette and quality notes, then check its review box. Green pixels contribute colour; amber pixels were excluded. The material scene does not change during analysis or review.
5. Review the organ coverage and apply all reviewed colours together. The default strength is **65%**, using **Colour only**, which retains each organ's authored perceptual lightness. Multiple observations for one organ are pooled; one image is not silently allowed to replace another.

Missing organs retain their authored colours by default. **Borrow related tissue colours** adds a conservative adjustment within explicitly declared tissue families. **Harmonize other soft organs** extends a smaller colour shift to the remaining soft structures while retaining their distinct baseline colours and lightness. With the full bundled set and both options, the plan covers all **36 soft structures**; the **three skeletal structures remain unchanged**. Coverage labels distinguish **direct**, **related**, **harmonized** and **authored** assignments. A harmonized heart or lung is not presented as photographed heart or lung tissue.

**Restore previous colours** restores the exact palette and plan preceding the latest batch application. An individual organ edit invalidates that undo operation, removes only that organ from the batch plan, and retains the other organs' source evidence. **Download colour plan** saves the applied plan if one exists, otherwise the reviewed proposal; the interface states which is being saved. The JSON preserves contributing source/mask hashes, observed palettes, quality summaries, surface identity, source attribution, grouping and inference basis. It does not embed uploaded images or regenerate segmentation without the original images/masks. There is no whole-scene project importer in this change.

## Sources and accuracy boundaries

The **Dresden Surgical Anatomy Dataset (DSAD)** provides original surgical RGB images and final masks reviewed by a physician following multiple annotations and consensus processing. We use those semantic annotations for the included library, rather than asking a generic image segmenter to invent labels. The selected organs are **liver, stomach, spleen, pancreas, small intestine and colon**. Kidney, gallbladder, heart and lung photographs are **not included** in this starter set. The photographs represent actual surgical appearances; they are not a certified healthy-population atlas. [Dataset publication](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01719-2), [original data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.21702600), [acquisition and source review](research/body-reference-sources-2026-10-08.md).

All six RGB photographs and seven binary masks are copied without recolouring, cropping or recompression. Attribution, exact repository revision, source rows and SHA256 hashes accompany the [CC BY 4.0 assets](../apps/studio/public/body-reference/README.md). They total approximately 8.22 MB. Six images come from five surgery groups; repeated observations are not independent patients. The exposed pancreatic surface is labelled uncertain/mixed and cannot drive inferred colours for other organs.

The refreshed model investigation found that **Surgical SAM 3.1 weights are publicly available**. Its model card nevertheless reports substantial variation in organ mask performance; it does not establish reliable automatic labelling for arbitrary uploaded surgical photographs. We corrected the earlier broad unavailable-weights statement. Spatial SAM2 prompts remain available for user-labelled regions; the included sources use their original reviewed masks. No claim is made that SAM2 universally outperforms SAM3. [Updated segmentation research](research/surgical-segmentation-2026-10-08.md).

## Colour method

The existing estimator rejects mask boundaries, deep shadow, glare/clipping and robust chromatic outliers, then estimates central colour and three swatches in Oklab. The whole-body planner adds duplicate suppression, bounded quality weighting and weighted-median pooling across reviewed observations, first within an image, then within a declared capture group, and finally across groups. Repeated frames from one known surgery cannot accumulate independent votes. Uploads without capture metadata fall back to image hashes; those hashes cannot identify related frames or patients. Weights describe sample usability, not probabilities of correct anatomy. Disagreement is reported rather than hidden by an unqualified average.

Related and whole-body adjustments transfer a bounded **difference from the source organ's authored colour**, not its absolute RGB colour. Their maximum pre-strength Oklab chroma shifts are respectively **0.035** and **0.020**. Only reviewed outer-surface evidence can drive these inferred assignments. They retain target lightness, use hue-preserving gamut compression and exclude bone. The default leaves these inferences off. These bounds are engineering choices tested for predictable behaviour, not measured physiological limits. [Detailed algorithm, current primary research and mapping table](research/whole-body-appearance-2026-10-08.md).

The photographs contain surgical lighting, camera colour processing, shadows, instruments and sometimes exposed surfaces. No white chart or known-reflectance target is present. We therefore do not estimate calibrated albedo, roughness or subsurface scattering, and do not project photographed vessel layouts onto unrelated anatomy. The existing organ-specific spatial texture, relief and finish remain authored. Published inverse-rendering approaches require additional geometry, poses or same-scene video. [Material research](research/surgical-appearance-2026-10-08.md), [EndoPBR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Han_EndoPBR_Photorealistic_Synthetic_Data_for_Surgical_3D_Vision_via_Physically-based_WACV_2026_paper.html).

## Implementation and evidence

`POST /api/atlas/reference-material/analyze-regions` accepts one base64 photograph, a segmentation method, and up to twelve explicit tissue regions with optional reviewed masks. It returns per-region analyses or errors and a common source-image hash. The existing 12 MiB image limit, 24-megapixel decode limit, 32 MiB request limit and shared single-analysis lock remain in effect. The browser queues up to eight images sequentially and rejects stale responses after edits/cancellation. Source photographs are processed in memory by the configured anatomy API; no external inference service receives them.

The planner prepares a complete proposal before one atomic palette/provenance state update. The viewport replaces only changed material instances and records the complete plan in scene metadata. Geometry buffers, physics state, camera and lighting are independent of this update. Legacy reference-colour hashes identify a representative contributing image/mask; **the full plan is necessary to audit pooled or inferred colour**, since the single compatibility record is not a measurement of its derived target.

Seven actual original-mask extraction calls succeeded, retaining 39.5–78.3% of selected pixels. The shadowed liver region triggered the low-retention warning, leading to addition of the brighter view, which retained 78.3%. The final source masks and accepted-pixel overlays were visually inspected. These checks establish execution and sampling behaviour, not independent segmentation accuracy or clinical validity. [Source review, overlays and measured results](../artifacts/whole-body-colour-2026-10-08/source-review/).

The upload workflow also ran SAM2.1 on the brighter liver photograph with a box and one positive tissue point. At the analyser's 1024 × 819 resolution, comparison with the original reviewed mask gave **IoU 0.9648 / Dice 0.9821** and a maximum absolute observed-sRGB channel difference of **0.01184**. This is one development smoke comparison, not a held-out segmentation benchmark or automatic organ-recognition score. The overlay excluded the video controls and surrounding tissue; the colour estimator excluded the brightest patch and darker peripheral areas. [Actual response, overlay and comparison metrics](../artifacts/whole-body-colour-2026-10-08/final-browser/whole-body-color-uploaded--c313b-s-explicitly-reviewed-organ/).

Verification of the final integration is recorded in [acceptance](ACCEPTANCE.md). Tests cover distinct regions sharing one image, partial errors, bounded work, duplicate source handling, surface mismatch, organ mapping, pooling, inferred-shift limits, review gating and preservation of the scene. Engineering visual review checks mask leakage, contamination, retained organ differences and before/after appearance. No surgeon validation or automatic semantic accuracy is claimed.
