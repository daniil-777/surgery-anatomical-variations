# Studio motion experiments: integration and evidence plan

Audit date: 2026-10-07. This is a design and code audit, not an implemented motion feature or a tissue validation result. No renderer, solver, dataset, or frozen model files were changed for this audit.

**Recommended first release:** a separate, reproducible motion experiment attached to a frozen Atlas specimen: reference-video playback, prescribed or measured tool trajectory, explicit camera alignment, replayed fitted motion, and held-out tracking errors. Expose physical material estimates only after the experiment establishes metric scale, forces or other identifiable loading, boundary conditions, and model validity. The same interface can cover all 12 organs; the available evidence and simulation domain must remain organ- and experiment-specific.

Exterior residuals can imply extrapolating weights: record mapping distances and bounds, verify rest reconstruction and rigid-motion reproduction, and do not mistake that check for a valid anatomical volume.

## Minimum experiment contract and UI

Proposed artifact `MotionExperiment` should include:

- Immutable specimen identity and full original recipe, including shape, disease, material, seeds, source attribution, and template transforms. Bind any cage/embedding to the *realized* disease-modified reference surface.
- Evidence kind: `reference_playback`, `video_fitted_motion_proxy`, `synthetic_solver_preview`, or `force_calibrated_model`. These are distinct claims, not automatic promotion levels.
- Video provenance/license, file hash, specimen/species/organ, native frame timestamps, selected interval, crop/resize transform, and train/validation/test grouping. A generic Atlas organ and a different video specimen form an illustrative comparison until initial geometry correspondence is established.
- Camera intrinsics, distortion handling, stereo baseline where available, camera motion, and a named transform chain between camera, tool, force sensor, specimen, and renderer. Store units explicitly: renderer millimetres; mechanics metres, seconds, newtons. Unknown transforms remain unknown.
- Tool poses/displacements with timestamps and measured-versus-inferred labels; loading and support masks; contact geometry/assumptions; force/wrench origin, expression frame, calibration/tare/gravity compensation and synchronization when available. Rotating a force vector is insufficient to move a full wrench between origins: torque also needs the moment-arm term.
- Solver/proxy identity, parameter names and units, fitted/fixed flags, bounds, confidence or sensitivity results, fit objective, excluded/occluded observations, held-out residuals, solver settings, and failure status. Store correspondence confidence separately from material uncertainty.
- Replay state/checkpoints and per-frame geometry hashes; capture camera, lighting, size, codec, timing, and environment metadata.

The initial panel should offer a reference video and Studio viewport side by side, shared play/pause/frame-step/scrub controls, frame time, tool path, optional observed-versus-predicted point overlays, and a compact evidence label. Show loading/holding/release intervals only if annotated or detected with recorded confidence. Scrubbing restores a checkpoint and advances fixed simulation steps; it never integrates backward or uses render-frame duration as physical time.

All organs may use reference playback and, when available, an explicitly kinematic fitted displacement field. A surface displacement basis with temporal coefficients is a **motion proxy**; its amplitude/spatial extent/temporal coefficients are not Young's modulus, viscosity, or clinical tissue properties. Avoid invented coefficients before a fit contract and data exist. Enable volumetric simulation only for a validated reference domain, embedding, supports, and loading setup. Cardiac contraction, insufflated hollow organs, perfusion, friction, and cutting require specific models and evidence; a generic grab is not coverage of these mechanisms.

Starting an experiment freezes the committed specimen. New shape/disease/seed generation stops playback and invalidates the bound fit/cage; retain the old experiment as a separate artifact. Appearance changes can be replayed only as explicitly versioned presentation changes, never as improved motion accuracy. Preserve rest-coordinate texture/disease mapping, recompute deformed normals, update bounds or use conservative deformation bounds, and restore the exact original surface on exit. Existing GLB export continues exporting the static committed specimen. A separately named experiment export contains replay/fit evidence; an optional deformed frame export must state its time and reference identity.

## Identifiability and evaluation

**Engineering inference:** under purely prescribed-displacement, quasi-static loading with fixed Poisson ratio and no other force scale, a homogeneous elastic energy of the form `E × W0` has the same displacement minimizer for every positive E. Camera fit alone cannot identify E in that setup. Known forces, known inertial/gravitational loading with appropriate mass and timing, or another calibrated observation can add information. Unknown contact, boundaries, camera motion, metric scale, and tissue prestress must not be absorbed into a misleading stiffness estimate. A low video residual is insufficient evidence of uniquely recovered mechanics.

Fit a minimal identifiable parameter subset and test sensitivity/multiple initializations. Hold out complete loading trajectories and specimens, not adjacent video frames. Compare to static, rigid-motion, simple kinematic, and appropriate solver baselines. Report initial-shape/camera registration error separately from subsequent deformation error. Disease morphology does not automatically assign disease-specific mechanical properties.

| Evidence | Report | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Video tracking | Visible-point endpoint error in pixels; calibrated stereo error in mm only when justified; threshold accuracy, visibility/occlusion coverage, silhouette overlap, held-out trajectories, per-specimen uncertainty. | Internal strain, contact forces, constitutive parameters, or clinical realism. Do not score self-generated pseudo-labels as independent ground truth. |
| Deformation consistency | Surface fold/self-intersection checks; cage min J and volume drift when a valid volume exists; embedding errors; rejected steps; recovery displacement. | Tissue validity. Positive cage J does not prove that an extrapolated render surface is nonintersecting, or that the physics is correct. |
| Mechanical validation | Held-out force–displacement, relaxation/hysteresis, reaction forces, boundary-conditioned displacements and internal target errors; loading range, sampling, uncertainty, mesh/time convergence. | Transfer to unmeasured organs, diseases, species, loading rates, or in-vivo conditions. Numerical derivative tests verify implementation, not material truth. |
| Visual review | Blinded reviewer ratings of motion, tool interaction and appearance, with reference conditions and inter-reviewer disagreement. | Quantitative mechanics. Keep this outcome separate from tracking scores and functional software QA. |

## Rendering, performance, and comparison video

Keep render meshes stable; update existing `BufferAttribute` arrays and set `needsUpdate`. Run fitting offline and replay positions, or run a coarse validated cage in a worker using transferable double buffers. Preserve material rest attributes during either path. The current JS solver allocates position/velocity copies and constraint arrays each step, uses per-edge/per-tet temporary vectors, and computes metrics with spread reductions; benchmark before high-resolution use. Record solver, embedding, normal update, GPU render, and end-to-end p50/p95/p99 latency plus peak memory, mesh sizes, hardware, and dropped frames. Do not claim real time from average FPS alone.

Current screenshot and camera metadata APIs are suitable starting points. They do not expose a calibrated projection matrix, distortion or frame-timed buffer update. Add these through an experiment adapter rather than changing the frozen surface/material model. Existing static metadata says `geometry_modified: false`; motion captures must explicitly distinguish the unchanged reference artifact from the deformed display buffers and their new normals. Lock camera/exposure and capture size during comparison; the usual auto-fit/orbit view is an inspection view, not video registration.

For convenient browser recording, composite decoded reference video and rendered output into one fixed-size canvas, including frame/time/evidence labels; use `captureStream()` plus a supported `MediaRecorder` format. Origin-clean media is required. `captureStream(0)` and `requestFrame()` control when frames are requested but do not by themselves guarantee an offline constant-rate encoder timeline. See [W3C Media Capture from DOM Elements](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-fromelement/). Use decoded video `mediaTime` and `presentedFrames` for interactive synchronization and missed-frame detection, not `timeupdate` events; see [MDN requestVideoFrameCallback](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLVideoElement/requestVideoFrameCallback).

For evaluation evidence, decode exact reference frames with timestamps, advance replay to the designated times, explicitly render, save PNGs plus per-frame JSON/hash records, then encode a comparison video with a fixed frame schedule. Preserve original timestamps and any resampling policy. Browser wall-clock recording is a convenience preview; frame exports and raw metrics are the reproducible evidence. Validate first/last frames, frame count, seek/reset reproducibility, synchronization error, and labels in the encoded output.

## Primary evaluation papers and what to adopt

1. **Schmidt et al., STIR, IEEE TMI 2024.** Infrared ICG markings supply tissue correspondences that are not visible to ordinary RGB trackers; the paper evaluates 2D/3D endpoint tracking. Use its separation of tracking observations and appearance. Limitations include temporally sparse start/end labels, switching-related timing uncertainty, porcine in-vivo rather than human procedures, and limited anatomical coverage. This is tracking evidence, not recorded tissue force/material ground truth. [Paper and full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16782v2), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2024.3372828).

2. **Schmidt et al., STIRC2025, July 2026 preprint.** The challenge assesses 2D and 3D threshold accuracy and computational latency, including tail latency; its discussion emphasizes cross-validation. Adopt visibility-aware interpretation, fixed hardware reporting and held-out sequences. Endpoints used for scoring are unoccluded, even though occlusions may occur between them; this does not supply continuous deformation truth or validate material laws. The paper's latency aggregation descriptions differ between the metrics and results sections, so pin the released evaluator/version before claiming exact challenge equivalence. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12939v1), [authors' evaluator](https://github.com/athaddius/STIRMetrics).

3. **Cartucho et al., SurgT, Medical Image Analysis 91, 2024.** Provides 157 stereo videos from 20 clinical cases with stereo calibration; methods track tissue bounding boxes and are ranked using Expected Average Overlap. Useful for clinical tracking robustness and coarse ROI baselines. Bounding-box overlap cannot establish dense strain, patient-specific stiffness, or contact realism. Keep case-level splits and camera calibration, and do not present its tracking task as a force-validation benchmark. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03022), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2023.102985).

4. **Heiselman et al., Image-to-Physical Liver Registration Sparse Data Challenge, JMI 2024.** Compares methods on 112 registration scenarios from a silicone liver phantom with 159 subsurface targets, evaluating target registration error and deformation-field consistency. Adopt internal held-out targets and sensitivity to observed surface coverage; fitting the visible surface is not enough. This is controlled phantom registration, not validation of human material properties or all-organ dynamics. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10773576/), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JMI.11.1.015001).

5. **Lim et al., in-situ human cadaver tissue measurements, Surgical Endoscopy 2009 (online 2008).** Measures liver/stomach force response to controlled indentation in ten fresh cadavers, including ramp/hold and sinusoidal protocols, and fits elastic/viscoelastic descriptions. Adopt synchronized force/displacement, preconditioning, explicit contact geometry, loading ranges and held-out response curves. Cadaver state, uninsufflated stomach, tested rates/depths, and only two organs limit transfer; fitted population averages must not become universal defaults. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693244/), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-008-0154-z).

## Implementation gate

First inspect the selected clip's rights, actual image timestamps, calibration, tool-pose frame and force/wrench metadata. The candidate ex-vivo chicken recording reported by the data audit may support playback and tracking; an empty wrench frame and missing transform are unresolved measurement provenance, not permission to assume camera-aligned forces. Freeze a legitimate motion-proxy or calibrated-model contract before adding a fitting UI. Then implement playback/capture, establish held-out baselines, add only the fit supported by those observations, and demonstrate identity-preserving reset/export plus real browser replay. No new organ stiffness claims follow merely from adding the panel.
